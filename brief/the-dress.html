The Dress

A reference for my girls — gowns worth a look.

The vision · an allover lace gown with a corset bodice, sleeves that open and move instead of hugging the arm, and a fitted-to-flared or A-line skirt, designed for an open-air July afternoon in the mountains of the Dominican Republic.

The Guide

Each category, with the reasoning behind it.

Fabric

Breathable first, for an open-air July afternoon.

  • Allover lace is the foundation because it is timeless, classic, and breathable in summer heat.
  • Light linings keep the lace breathable, so tulle or mesh is preferred over heavy crepe or satin underlays.
  • Lace types are flexible, and both fine Chantilly with eyelash edges and textured corded or Alençon lace are worth comparing.
  • Tulle skirt layers are welcome for the same reason: they are lightweight and breathable.

Corset

Structure that supports without shouting.

  • A corset is required to support the bust and midsection.
  • Placement is flexible, with a built-in corset, an overlay corset worn over the skirt, and a dropped-V waist seam that suggests a corset line all worth trying.
  • Boning visibility is flexible, with hidden boning, lace-covered tonal boning, and exposed sheer boning all worth comparing on a full bust.
  • Bust support is flexible, with molded cups, soft sewn-in cups, and a cupless internal corset all worth trying.

Sleeves

Covered arms, with room to move.

  • Sleeves are required to cover the arms fully, in lace dense enough to soften a tattoo sleeve beneath.
  • Length is flexible, and full-length sleeves reaching the wrist or hand are welcome.
  • Shoulder placement is flexible, from off the shoulder, to on the shoulder, to higher toward the neck while keeping an open neckline.
  • The flare point is flexible, from a cuff at the wrist, to a trumpet flare from the forearm, to a bell flare from the elbow, to an angel sleeve that opens along the arm, as long as the sleeve opens somewhere instead of staying snug to the wrist.

Neckline

Open, and cool enough for July.

  • Sweetheart variations lead the list, including the classic sweetheart, the sweetheart with an eyelash lace edge, and the inverted sweetheart.
  • Strapless scalloped necklines are also worth trying for their soft lace edge.
  • An open neckline is required to stay cool in July heat.

Silhouette

Fitted to flared, tried every way.

  • Trumpet is the primary direction because it defines curves while leaving room to move and dance.
  • A-line is a strong second, especially when paired with an overlay corset.
  • Mermaid is worth trying to see how a fit through the knee reads before ruling it in or out.

Comparison Only

One of each, to be sure.

  • A bolero is worth one try to see the look, although it is not the leading direction.
  • A full ballgown is worth one try to confirm or rule out volume.
  • A subtle sparkle lace is worth one try to test how shine reads.
  • A 3D floral appliqué is worth one try to test how raised texture reads.

Not of Interest

Ruled out, with the reason.

  • Satin, mikado, and other heavy fabrics are ruled out because they do not breathe and they damage easily.
  • Detachable sleeves and arm gauntlets are ruled out in favor of sleeves built into the gown.
  • High necklines and mock necks are ruled out because of the heat.
  • Heavy rhinestone or crystal embellishment is ruled out in favor of the lace doing the work.

Veil

Still open.

  • The veil is still to be decided and will follow once the dress direction is set.
